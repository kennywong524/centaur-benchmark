\paragraph{Autonomous model evaluation.}
General benchmarks such as MMLU, BIG-Bench, and HELM standardized broad
comparisons of model knowledge, reasoning, and robustness
\citep{hendrycks2021,srivastava2023,liang2023}. Newer evaluations increase
realism by using industrial workflows, occupational tasks, and professional
deliverables \citep{sopbench2025,gdpval2025,profbench2025,occubench2026,
jobbench2026}. This progression makes automation evaluation more relevant to
deployment, but the model remains responsible for the final output. It does
not measure how much value the same model creates when another agent executes
the task.

\paragraph{Interactive assistance.}
Field experiments show that generative AI can improve writing, programming,
consulting, and customer-support performance
\citep{noy2023,peng2023,dellacqua2023,brynjolfsson2025}, while other evidence
shows that human--AI combinations can underperform the better party alone
\citep{vaccaro2024}. These results establish that assistance value depends on
the task, worker, and interaction design. Their goal, however, is usually to
estimate the effect of access to one system, not to compare several candidate
assistants while holding the worker and task fixed.

Related multi-agent work studies stronger models teaching weaker models
\citep{saha2023}, planning for weaker code generators \citep{gandhi2025}, and
LLM tutoring quality \citep{macina2025}. These studies demonstrate that
solving and assisting can require different capabilities, but focus on a
single domain or interaction type. Similarly, HAPI evaluates human involvement
in paid agent projects, but places the human in the assisting role
\citep{upwork_hapi2025}. Our setting reverses that direction: the model under
evaluation supplies guidance to a fixed worker.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
\citet{haupt2025} call for ``centaur evaluations'' that measure how AI
improves another party's performance rather than only imitating its output,
and \citet{fugener2026} formalize why automation and augmentation may have
different returns. \textsc{CentaurBench} operationalizes this distinction as
a controlled benchmark: the same model set is evaluated in both roles, on the
same professional tasks, with the worker held fixed. This design enables the
cross-mode rank comparison that autonomous benchmarks and single-system field
experiments do not provide.
